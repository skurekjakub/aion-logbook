# AION 2: Best Macro Setup Guide

Home›Guides›AION 2: Best Macro Setup Guide

Updated Sep 30, 2026Read Time 4 min

Written byArgie MaputiSenior Game Guide Writer

Share

Quick Answer

You want to build macros around repeatable damage skills with a 10 ms delay and keep defensive, burst, charged, and situational abilities separate. Also, use the built-in macro system instead of external hardware macros.

Full guide below ↓

The macro system in AION 2 can make combat considerably easier, especially when you’re juggling several skills with short animation windows all at once. However, you don’t just put as many abilities as possible into one button and consider it the optimal setup.

This is especially considering recent balance changes have specifically targeted macro-dependent skill cancels. As such, you as a player should be more selective about what you automate.

To help you with that, we have created this guide to show you the best macro setup you can possibly use.

## How Does AION 2’s Macro System Work?

Before anything else, you should first understand what the game actually allows you to do.

The game’s built-in macro system is essentially a sequence of your existing hotbar skills. You assign the macro to a key, add the skills you want it to activate, then hold that key during combat. This allows you to execute those skills in a sequence instead of pressing each of their hotkey individually.

To find the macro keybind, head to Settings > Key Settings > General > Gameplay > Macro. The actual macro editor is located in the Skill window. Since the macro isn’t necessarily assigned to a key automatically, make sure you bind one before testing it.

The most important setting to consider is the Delay between entries. Current macro guides generally use 10 ms, which is also the lowest commonly available setting. This delay is the gap between the macro’s inputs.

It’s worth noting that there’s a lot of conflicting advice surrounding macro delay, especially since players often confuse delay with network latency.

The macro delay determines how quickly the game moves from one input in the sequence to the next. It doesn’t mean your ping is converted into milliseconds. If your setup regularly skips abilities or behaves inconsistently, I’d recommend testing a slightly longer delay to see if it fixes things.

The easiest way to determine whether your macro is working properly is to use a training target and compare the damage and skill usage with your normal manual rotation.

## The Best General Macro Setup You Can Use

To build your macro, you have to divide your skills into three groups.

First, put your repeatable damage skills inside the macro. These are abilities you want to use whenever they become available. These are also skills that don’t require you to make a decision based on the situation.

Second, keep situational abilities outside it. These could include gap closers, defensive cooldowns, crowd-control abilities, and major burst skills.

Third, don’t automatically place every buff inside the macro. Toggle-style abilities can cause problems since activating them a second time may turn them off.

That said, one of the categories you shouldn’t put in a macro is charged abilities. That’s because if a skill becomes stronger the longer you hold or charge it, a repeating macro may activate it before you’ve reached the desired charge level.

Doing so can only turn a powerful ability into an underpowered version just because the macro moved to its next command.

The same principle applies to abilities where timing matters more than raw frequency. If you need to watch a boss mechanic, debuff, positioning requirement, or specific buff window before using a skill, leave it on a separate key.

## What About Hardware Macros?

Some Korean players have combined the game’s built-in macro with Logitech, Razer, or other mouse/keyboard macro software. With this, the in-game macro handles one part of the rotation while an external hardware macro repeatedly activates the other skills.

However, I don’t recommend using that approach.

As you can see, NCSoft’s recent balance changes have specifically targeted gameplay that relies on extremely precise automated skill-cancel timing. For instance, they’ve increased the cancel delay on Templar’s Pummel + Judgment and Assassin’s Savage Roar + Heart Gore combinations.

This change was specifically designed to reduce the advantage provided by mouse-macro timing. However, they compensated for this nerf by adjusting damage to the affected skills, but only in a PvE environment.

As such, try to stick to the game’s built-in system as much as possible.

## Final Thoughts

The macro system in AION 2 is actually pretty useful since it takes some of the repetitive button pressing out of combat. However, you’ll have to set things up for yourself to see which works best for your playstyle.

That said, as long as you follow the suggestions above, you should be able to find the perfect setup eventually.
